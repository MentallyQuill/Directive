## Fair Discovery and Flexible Investigation

The direct discovery path is:

```text
known Hecate route and hazard
    -> actual defensible access or a recorded access cost
    -> actual sensor, physical, or technical contact
    -> required report: relay and human-archive character
    -> recovered system evidence
    -> required report: bounded distributed architecture
    -> recovered logs, traffic records, handshakes, or equivalent direct evidence
    -> required report: access history and Demeris/Mira next lead
```

The player does not have to use a particular prose style, order a named scan, board the site, accuse a person, or discover a hidden controller. Probe analysis, away-team work, remote extraction, traffic reconstruction, testimony, negotiation, or another credible equivalent can satisfy the same authored evidence standards. The model classifies accepted prose against those standards; deterministic policy, source, prerequisite, and revision checks own state changes.

Direct evidence loss does not dead-end the campaign. Responsible withdrawal, forced loss of access, destruction without a usable copy, loss or seizure, and leaving the site unresolved can activate an alternate corroboration route through established Prel or Cardassian records, Compact testimony, traffic residue, or another credible source. That route can preserve the Demeris/Mira lead while recording partial evidence rather than fabricating the missing system or architecture facts.

The next lead is deliberately bounded. The mission may establish local postwar access, a current Starfleet Intelligence handshake, and a Demeris maintenance account associated with Mira Solenn. It may not turn those facts into proof that Pale Lantern, Farwatch, Holt, Rourke, Wayward Sun, or any other actor controls the wider operation.

## Player Ownership, No-Fault Loss, and Mixed Outcomes

The player's physical-system and human-material choices are exclusively user-sourced. Assistant prose cannot decide to isolate, observe, destroy, withdraw, release, restrict, share, or transfer custody for the player. Actual access, containment, destruction, exposure, loss, release, and custody results are assistant/runtime/adjudicator observations and cannot be self-certified by player assertion.

One explicit withdrawal message may record both responsible withdrawal and leaving the inaccessible system in place. This coupled handling avoids requiring a second artificial command while preserving the stronger discovery prerequisite for every other system choice.

Failure after informed action requires both:

- player knowledge of the system's character; and
- an actual recorded player decision before the adverse result.

If the system or archive is lost before the player could make an informed choice, the mission records a serious cost and campaign consequence, not player failure. A later report may explain what was lost, but it does not retroactively blame the player. Conversely, controlled observation that exposes protected parties or a chosen custody course that loses control can be recorded as `failedAfterInformedAction` once both physical and human results are known.

The mission supports these deterministic terminal dispositions:

- `accountableIsolation` for preserved isolation with protected joint or restricted custody;
- `boundedObservation` for contained observation with accountable archive custody;
- `costlyTruthForward` when usable truth survives an access, destruction, release, or opaque-custody cost;
- `privacyProtectedEvidenceLost` when immediate denial protects privacy but destroys direct evidence;
- `responsibleWithdrawalForward` when the player avoids an unjustified hazard and preserves an alternate route;
- `compromisedCustodyForward` for forced access failure, exposure, seizure, or lost archive control.

All dispositions continue the campaign. Missing evidence changes confidence, cost, and consequences; it does not require the player to guess an undisclosed plot action.

## Non-Linear State and Closure

Evidence, custody, and access can advance in different causal orders. The fixtures prove custody before full analysis and the next lead before the architecture report. Delivering the lead early does not freeze the evidence objective at partial success; partial completion becomes terminal only after a real evidence-loss condition makes the remaining direct route unavailable.

Player choices alone do not resolve custody. Both the physical-system result and the human-material result must be established before custody can terminalize. This prevents a transition packet from being committed while a consequential result is still pending.

The mission closes only after all three required objectives have an authored terminal disposition. The access and evidence objectives distinguish clean completion, recorded cost, and responsible handoff. Custody distinguishes protected completion, deliberate cost, handoff, and failure after informed action. Internal events and raw outcomes remain hidden; the player-facing state is the three objectives, discovered aggregate facts, and four dimensions.

## Adversarial Review Findings

The implementation review found and fixed these Important non-UI issues:

1. The evidence objective could terminalize as partial as soon as the next lead arrived, preventing a later architecture report from upgrading it. Partial completion now requires a real evidence-loss condition, and a lead-before-analysis scenario proves non-linear recovery.
2. Custody failure could be assigned when the system was lost before the player knew its character or made a choice. Failure now requires knowledge plus a recorded decision; loss before choice records cost without blame.
3. Adverse custody could terminalize on the physical loss before the archive result existed, committing a stale transition. Both results must now be known before adverse custody closes.
4. A single player withdrawal message could not satisfy the relay-withdraw policy because proposal prerequisites were evaluated before the access decision in the same batch. The narrow withdrawal policy now accepts the coupled pending state without exposing other hidden choices.
5. Simultaneous physical and archive loss could reject the archive claim because it depended on a relay result from the same proposal. Archive loss is now independently grounded by established site contact, allowing one accepted scene to record both outcomes.
6. Archive non-recovery could similarly lag same-scene destruction or withdrawal. Its policy now accepts an already-recorded destructive decision or access disposition while retaining clear-outcome evidence standards.

The review also challenged report ordering, report cancellation, pending reports at closure, actor identity, controller over-attribution, direct versus alternate evidence, forced access, privacy-first destruction, controlled observation, broad release, Intelligence custody, choice/result separation, mixed outcomes, terminal priorities, no-clock semantics, legacy-state leakage, registry order, activation replay, source restoration, and descendant pruning. No unresolved Critical or Important non-UI finding remains in this slice.
