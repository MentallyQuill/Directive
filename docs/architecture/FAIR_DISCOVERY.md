# Fair Discovery

Directive separates world truth from player knowledge. A fact may exist in authored package data while remaining unavailable to the narrator's player-facing projection.

The Mission page is a projection of knowledge already established in the story, never a briefing channel for future hooks. Mission summaries and immediately visible objective copy may restate the opening assignment or accepted transition, but they must not name an incident, person, system, motive, target, or conclusion before accepted story state establishes it. An authored fact that is true but has not yet been narrated remains `discoverable`; authors must not mark it `known` merely to advertise it in the UI.

Important discoverable information needs at least one fair delivery route. A route names who can deliver it, what capability makes them credible, what circumstances make delivery appropriate, and what source-backed disclosure counts as knowledge. Captain Whitaker is deliberately available as an experienced autonomous guide, but she does not solve every problem or override player agency.

Duty Reports provide deterministic custody for important disclosures. A report can be pending, assigned to a qualified actor, visibly delivered in narration, accepted on the next player message, and then materialized as knowledge. If the selected response changes before acceptance, the report was never delivered. If its source is later invalidated, the associated knowledge and dependent mission state rebuild.

Players may receive positive consequences for exploring deeply, preserving evidence, making an informed choice, or completing optional work. They are not punished for missing facts they were never given a fair opportunity to know. Hidden optional content cannot secretly fail a mission.

Crew initiative is authored and bounded. Officers may warn, volunteer, disagree, recommend, or escalate in character. They are story participants, not tutorial pop-ups or automatic answer dispensers.
